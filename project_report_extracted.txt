CHAPTER ONE 
GENERAL INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Introduction 
Waste management has become a critical environmental and public health concern, particularly 
in rapidly urbanizing regions. In many developing countries, including Cameroon, the increasing 
rate of population growth and urban expansion has led to a significant rise in solid waste 
generation. However, waste collection and management systems have not evolved at the same 
pace, resulting in inefficient waste disposal practices and environmental degradation. 
In major urban centers such as Buea and Douala, waste management is often handled through 
centralized systems operated by municipal councils and private contractors. Despite these efforts, 
many areas still experience irregular waste collection, accumulation of garbage in public spaces, 
and poor sanitation conditions. These challenges are largely attributed to limitations in 
infrastructure, lack of real-time monitoring systems, and inefficient communication between 
residents and waste management authorities. 
Traditional waste management approaches in Cameroon typically rely on fixed collection 
schedules and manual reporting mechanisms. As a result, waste collection services are often 
unable to respond promptly to dynamic conditions such as overflowing bins or illegal dumping. 
This leads to health risks, environmental pollution, and reduced quality of life for residents. 
Advances in digital technology have introduced new opportunities to improve waste 
management through the development of smart waste management systems. These systems 
leverage modern technologies such as web applications, cloud computing, geographic 
information systems (GIS), and route optimization algorithms to enhance waste collection 
efficiency. By integrating real-time reporting mechanisms and intelligent scheduling techniques, 
smart waste management systems can ensure that waste is collected more efficiently and that 
collection resources are allocated appropriately. 
The increasing adoption of smart city technologies has further encouraged the use of digital 
platforms to monitor urban infrastructure and services. Within this context, waste management 
6 
 
systems can be improved by integrating digital reporting tools that allow residents to notify 
waste management authorities when garbage bins are full. 
This project proposes the design and development of a smart waste management and garbage 
pickup scheduling system, a web-based platform designed to improve waste collection 
efficiency by enabling resident reporting, intelligent scheduling, and route optimization. The 
system aims to enhance communication between residents and waste management authorities 
while supporting more efficient waste collection planning. 
Through the implementation of this system, it is expected that waste collection operations can 
become more responsive, data-driven, and transparent, thereby contributing to improved 
sanitation and environmental sustainability within communities such as Buea. 
1.2 Background of the Study 
Effective waste management is a critical component of environmental sustainability and public 
health. Rapid urbanization, population growth, and increased consumption have significantly 
contributed to the rise in municipal solid waste generation across many developing countries. 
Cities are increasingly struggling to cope with the volume of waste produced daily, which places 
pressure on existing waste management infrastructures and operational systems [1]. 
In many developing countries, including Cameroon, waste management services are largely 
managed by municipal authorities in partnership with sanitation companies such as HYSACAM 
(Hygiene and Sanitation of Cameroon). These institutions are responsible for waste collection, 
transportation, and disposal within urban communities. Despite these efforts, waste accumulation 
remains a persistent issue in several cities and towns due to logistical challenges, limited 
infrastructure, financial constraints, and rapid urban expansion [2]. 
Studies indicate that urban areas in Cameroon generate an average of 0.6 to 0.8 kilograms of 
solid waste per person per day, resulting in thousands of tons of waste produced daily in major 
cities [13], [14]. For instance, the city of Douala alone generates over 2,000 tons of waste per 
day, while Yaoundé produces approximately 1,500 tons daily [14]. Despite these figures, only a 
7 
 
portion of this waste is effectively collected and properly disposed of, leaving a significant 
amount uncollected in residential areas, streets, and drainage systems. 
In areas such as Buea, waste accumulation is frequently observed in residential neighborhoods, 
roadside locations, markets, and commercial areas. Garbage bins may overflow before scheduled 
pickup times, and certain locations may experience irregular waste collection services. Residents 
often lack a structured communication channel to report full bins, which further delays response 
from waste management authorities. Consequently, these inefficiencies contribute to 
environmental pollution, unpleasant odors, blockage of drainage systems, and increased risk of 
disease outbreaks [3]. 
Traditional waste collection methods in Cameroon rely heavily on fixed schedules and manual 
monitoring. Waste collection trucks follow predetermined routes regardless of whether waste 
bins are full or not. This approach leads to inefficiencies such as missed collections, overflowing 
bins, and unnecessary resource usage. In addition, there is limited communication between 
residents and waste management authorities, making it difficult to report urgent waste-related 
issues in real time [4]. 
In recent years, the emergence of digital technologies has enabled the development of smart 
waste management systems designed to improve the efficiency of waste collection processes. 
These systems integrate technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT), geographic 
information systems (GIS), cloud computing, and mobile/web applications to monitor waste 
containers, analyze collection data, and optimize garbage collection routes [5]. 
IoT-enabled waste management systems utilize sensors embedded within smart bins to monitor 
fill levels in real time. When bins reach a predefined capacity threshold, the sensors transmit data 
to a centralized monitoring system, which then schedules collection operations accordingly. This 
approach ensures that waste is collected only when necessary, thereby improving operational 
efficiency and reducing fuel consumption [6]. 
Another key technology used in modern waste management systems is route optimization 
algorithms, which determine the most efficient path for waste collection vehicles to follow when 
servicing multiple locations. These algorithms are often modeled using the Vehicle Routing 
8 
 
Problem (VRP) and related computational methods such as Dijkstra’s algorithm, A* search, and 
genetic algorithms. By calculating optimized routes, waste management authorities can reduce 
travel time, minimize operational costs, and improve overall service delivery [7]. 
Several developed cities around the world have already adopted smart waste management 
systems as part of broader smart city initiatives. These systems enable authorities to monitor 
waste levels in real time, allocate collection resources more efficiently, and maintain cleaner 
urban environments. However, such technologies are still limited in many developing regions 
due to infrastructure constraints, high implementation costs, and lack of technical integration [8]. 
In Cameroon, the adoption of fully automated IoT-based waste monitoring systems remains 
limited. Many communities continue to rely on manual reporting and fixed collection schedules, 
which often fail to address real-time waste management needs. Furthermore, residents generally 
lack a digital platform through which they can report waste accumulation or track collection 
activities. 
Therefore, there is a need for an intelligent, technology-driven waste management solution that 
improves communication between residents and waste management authorities while optimizing 
collection operations. This study proposes the design and development of a Smart Waste 
Management and Garbage Pickup Scheduling System, a web-based platform that allows 
residents to report waste accumulation while enabling administrators to schedule and optimize 
waste collection routes. 
By integrating resident reporting, automated scheduling mechanisms, and route optimization 
strategies, the proposed system aims to improve operational efficiency, reduce waste 
accumulation, and enhance sanitation management within communities. 
1.3 Statement of the Problem 
Despite the presence of waste management services provided by organizations such as 
HYSACAM, garbage accumulation remains a persistent challenge in urban communities such as 
Buea. Existing waste collection systems are often inefficient and unable to respond effectively to 
the dynamic nature of waste generation in these areas. 
9 
 
One major limitation of the current system is its reliance on fixed collection schedules and 
predetermined routes, regardless of the actual status of waste bins. As a result, some locations 
experience delayed or irregular waste collection, while others are serviced even when bins are 
not yet full. This leads to inefficient use of resources such as fuel, time, and manpower. In 
addition, there is no structured or reliable platform that allows residents to report overflowing 
bins or urgent waste-related issues in real time. 
The absence of real-time communication between residents and waste management authorities 
further worsens the situation, as issues may go unnoticed until scheduled collection times. 
Consequently, waste accumulation in residential areas, markets, and public spaces contributes to 
environmental pollution, unpleasant odors, blockage of drainage systems, and increased risk of 
disease outbreaks. 
These challenges highlight the limitations of traditional waste management approaches and 
demonstrate the need for a more responsive and efficient system. Therefore, there is a need for a 
smart, technology-driven waste management solution that enables real-time reporting, 
prioritization of waste collection based on urgency, and optimized routing of garbage collection 
vehicles.

This chapter reviews the theoretical foundations, key concepts, and existing research pertaining 
to smart waste management systems. It explores related technologies such as IoT (Internet of 
Things), route optimization, and intelligent scheduling methods. It also analyzes previous works

2.2 Review of Related Concepts or Conceptual Framework 
2.2.1 Smart Waste Management Systems 
Smart waste management systems have become an important component of modern smart city 
infrastructure because they enable real-time monitoring, efficient resource allocation, and 
improved sanitation management [8], [12]. 
Smart waste management systems use digital technologies such as sensors, embedded devices, 
cloud computing, and web/mobile platforms to monitor waste container status, analyze data, and 
automate collection activities [1]. These systems contrast with traditional manual collection by 
enabling real-time monitoring, efficient resource allocation, and reduced operational costs. 
Key Elements: 
 
Smart bins with sensors 
 
Data transmission 
 
Centralized servers for processing and scheduling 
 
Web or mobile dashboards for monitoring and alerts  
 
 
2.2.2 Internet of Things (IoT) in Waste Management 
14 
 
IoT technologies have significantly improved waste monitoring by allowing sensors embedded 
in bins to transmit fill-level data to centralized servers for analysis and scheduling [10]. 
IoT refers to networked physical devices that communicate data over the internet [2]. In waste 
management, IoT enables real-time reporting of bin status, automated alerts when bins are full, 
and dynamic route adjustments based on data trends. 
Common IoT components: 
 
Ultrasonic sensors for fill level 
 
GPS modules for location 
 
Microcontrollers for data collection 
 
Wireless communication 
2.2.3 Route Optimization and Scheduling Algorithms 
Waste collection routing is commonly modeled as a Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP), which 
aims to determine optimal routes for servicing multiple locations while minimizing operational 
costs [9]. 
Route optimization algorithms determine the most efficient sequence of bin pickups to minimize 
travel time and fuel consumption. This problem is commonly modeled as a variation of the 
Vehicle Routing Problem [3]. Scheduling combines server logic and priority scoring to decide 
which bins get collected first. 
Common techniques: 
 
Dijkstra’s Algorithm 
 
A* Search 
 
Genetic Algorithms 
 
Travelling Salesman Problem approximations 
Modern mapping platforms such as Google Maps Platform provide routing APIs that compute 
optimized paths using real-time traffic data [4]. 
15 
 
2.2.4 Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Mapping API Integration 
GIS enables spatial visualization of waste locations and optimized routes. Integration with 
mapping platforms (e.g., Google Maps API) allows real-time routing suggestions based on road 
data, traffic conditions, and vehicle position [2].

 
Authors: Praveen Kumar Naidu Rayanki & Bhavishya G 
 
Technology Used: IoT-enabled smart bins, Machine Learning algorithms, Vehicle

This study proposes an integrated smart waste management system combining IoT-enabled smart 
bins, machine learning-based waste classification, and route optimization for waste collection

recommends future improvements such as predictive scheduling, fault tolerance mechanisms, 
and large-scale city deployment.

which may increase implementation cost and complexity.

2.3.2 IoT Based Automated Smart Waste Management System

 
Authors: Pavithra M, Alagu Esakkiammal N, Angel Melbha A, et al 
 
Technology Used: Arduino Uno, GSM modem, ultrasonic sensors 
The study proposes an IoT-based smart dustbin designed to automate waste disposal and 
monitoring processes in urban environments. The system aims to address the increasing problem

practices. 
Unlike conventional dustbins that require manual opening and periodic inspection, the proposed 
smart dustbin integrates sensor-based circuitry to automate its operation. A clap or foot-tap 
detection sensor enables touchless opening of the bin lid, improving hygiene and user

detect when it is nearing capacity. 
When the garbage level reaches a predefined threshold, the system transmits real-time data via 
the internet to a central monitoring system. This notification alerts waste collectors to empty the 
specific bin before overflow occurs. The system also supports historical data tracking to analyze 
fill patterns over time, enabling improved long-term waste management planning.

mass production. The system is intended for use in homes, offices, and public spaces, aiming to

 
Project Name: Smart Waste Management System 
 
Authors: Ankit Kumar, Hritik Soni, Deepanshi Srivastava 
 
Technology Used: IoT sensors and cloud monitoring 
This study addresses the growing environmental and public health concerns associated with

a defined time margin after reaching the threshold, the system continues to display warnings to 
indicate delayed disposal. 
The main objective of the system is to prevent overflow, reduce environmental littering, and 
minimize health hazards caused by unattended waste bins. By ensuring timely waste collection, 
the system promotes improved environmental hygiene in smart city environments.

2.3.5 IoT-Based Smart Bin Allocation and Vehicle Routing 
 
Project Name: IoT-based Smart Bin Allocation and Vehicle Routing: A Case Study in 
South Korea 
 
Authors: ScienceDirect Research Article 
 
Technology Used: IoT, advanced neighborhood search routing algorithm 
20 
 
This study addresses the problem of increasing waste generation due to rapid urbanization and 
population growth. The authors highlight that irregular cleaning schedules and inefficient routing 
lead to bin overflow and poor waste management. 
The proposed system integrates IoT-enabled smart bins with a Central Monitoring System. The 
fill level of each bin is continuously monitored and transmitted to the Central Monitoring 
System, which then determines optimal waste collection routes. 
A key innovation introduced in this paper is the time-dependent penalty concept. Waste 
management authorities incur a penalty cost if bins are not emptied within a specified time after 
reaching full capacity. This ensures timely collection and discourages service delays. 
Additionally, the study introduces a neighbor-cleaning concept: when a filled bin is scheduled 
for cleaning, nearby bins may also be collected to improve operational efficiency. 
The algorithm’s effectiveness was validated using numerical experiments and benchmark 
instances from the Traveling Salesman Problem Library. Results were compared against standard 
Genetic Algorithm and traditional ACO approaches, demonstrating superior performance of the 
proposed VNS–ACO method.

 
Introduces a time-dependent penalty model for delayed collection. 
 
Includes sensitivity analysis for parameter validation. 


Limitations 
 
Heavy reliance on IoT infrastructure. 
 
Algorithm complexity may require high computational resources. 
 
Implementation feasibility in developing cities not deeply discussed.

2.4 Research Gap 
Although several studies have proposed smart waste management systems using IoT sensors, 
machine learning algorithms, and route optimization techniques, most existing solutions focus 
primarily on hardware-based monitoring of waste bins. These systems rely heavily on sensor 
infrastructure to detect fill levels and transmit data to central monitoring platforms. 
While such systems can effectively monitor bin status, they often require expensive hardware 
installations and stable connectivity infrastructure, which may not be feasible in many 
developing regions. Furthermore, many of these solutions do not incorporate user-driven 
reporting mechanisms that allow residents to directly notify waste management authorities of 
sanitation issues. 
Additionally, several studies emphasize route optimization algorithms for waste collection 
vehicles, but they typically assume the availability of real-time sensor data. In environments 
where IoT infrastructure is limited or unavailable, such approaches may not be practical. 
Therefore, there is a need for a waste management solution that: 
 
Does not rely heavily on IoT hardware  
 
Allows residents to actively report waste issues  
 
Supports intelligent scheduling of waste collection  
 
Provides route optimization for waste collection vehicles  
The proposed Smart Waste Management and Garbage Pickup Scheduling System aims to 
address these limitations by integrating resident reporting, intelligent scheduling, and route 
optimization within a single web-based platform designed for practical implementation in 
communities such as Buea. 
2.5 Proposed Solution 
The proposed Smart Waste Management and Garbage Pickup Scheduling System aims to 
address the limitations identified in existing waste management solutions by integrating digital 
reporting mechanisms with intelligent scheduling and routing techniques. 
22 
 
Unlike many existing systems that depend primarily on IoT-based smart bins, the proposed 
system emphasizes a community-driven reporting approach. Residents can submit reports 
through a web-based interface whenever garbage accumulation or overflowing bins are observed. 
This allows waste management authorities to receive real-time information about sanitation 
issues without requiring extensive sensor infrastructure. 
Once a report is submitted, the system stores the information in a centralized database and 
processes it through a scheduling module. The scheduling engine prioritizes waste collection

3.2 Development Methodology Used 
3.2.1 Incremental Development Methodology 
The Incremental Development Methodology was adopted for the development of the Smart 
Waste Management and Garbage Pickup Scheduling System. Incremental development is a 
software engineering approach in which a system is designed, implemented, and tested in small 
functional units known as increments. Each increment adds new functionality to the existing 
system until the complete application is developed [1]. 
The development of the system was divided into the following stages: 
 Requirement Gathering  
 System Analysis  
 System Design  
 Incremental Module Development  
 Testing and Validation  
 System Integration  
 Refinement and Documentation  
The Incremental Development Methodology was particularly suitable for this project because the 
system consists of several independent but related functional modules, including user 
25 
 
management, waste report management, task assignment and scheduling, notification 
management, route optimization, and administrative monitoring. Rather than developing the 
entire system at once, each module was implemented as a separate increment and tested before 
proceeding to the next stage. 
The incremental nature of the methodology enabled continuous verification and validation 
throughout the development process. Errors identified in one increment could be corrected 
before additional functionalities were introduced. For example, features such as duplicate report 
detection, location validation, and role-based access control were tested and refined during their 
respective development increments to improve system reliability and usability. 
This approach reduced development risks, improved software quality, and simplified debugging 
because problems could be isolated within specific increments rather than affecting the entire 
system. It also allowed gradual expansion of system functionality while maintaining a stable 
working version of the application throughout development. 
The Incremental Development Methodology was preferred over the Waterfall model because it 
supports the gradual delivery of functional modules and allows testing to be performed 
throughout the development process rather than only at the end of the project. This made it 
particularly suitable for a modular system such as the proposed Smart Waste Management and 
Garbage Pickup Scheduling System [6].

CHAPTER FOUR 
IMPLEMENTATION, RESULTS AND TESTING 
4.1 Introduction 
This chapter presents a comprehensive explanation of the implementation, results, and testing of 
the Smart Waste Management and Garbage Pickup Scheduling System. It provides a detailed 
account of how the system was developed based on the design specifications outlined in Chapter 
Three. Emphasis is placed on explaining how each component of the system was implemented, 
how the different modules interact, and how the system performs under testing conditions. The 
chapter also evaluates the system’s functionality and reliability through structured testing 
strategies. 
4.2 System Implementation 
The Smart Waste Management System was implemented as a web-based application using a 
three-tier architecture consisting of the presentation layer, application layer, and data layer. This 
architectural approach ensures separation of concerns, making the system easier to manage, 
maintain, and scale. 
The presentation layer consists of the user interface, which was developed using React plus 
TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS. This layer enables interaction between users and the system. The 
application layer, implemented using Node.js, handles the core logic of the system, including 
request processing, authentication, scheduling, and communication with the database. The data 
layer is managed using Supabase, which provides a PostgreSQL-based relational database for 
efficient data storage and retrieval. 
The backend server acts as the central processing unit of the system. It receives HTTP requests 
from the frontend, performs validation and business logic operations, interacts with the database, 
and returns appropriate responses to the client. 
4.2.1 Backend API Implementation 
The backend of the system was designed using a RESTful API architecture, where different 
endpoints are responsible for handling specific system operations. Each endpoint corresponds to 
39 
 
a particular functionality such as user registration, authentication, report submission, and task 
assignment. 
For instance, when a user submits a waste report, the frontend sends a POST request to the 
appropriate API endpoint. The backend receives this request, validates the input data, and inserts 
the report into the database. After successful insertion, the server responds with a confirmation 
message, which is then displayed to the user. 
This modular API structure ensures scalability, maintainability, and efficient communication 
between system components. 
4.2.2 Database Implementation and Interaction 
The database for the system was implemented using Supabase, which provides a relational 
database structure based on PostgreSQL. The database schema was defined using SQL migration 
scripts, ensuring that all tables, relationships, and constraints were properly established. 
The main tables in the system include Users, Reports, Assignments, and Notifications. These 
tables are interconnected using foreign key relationships, which ensure referential integrity 
within the system. 
When a user submits a report, the backend executes an SQL INSERT operation to store the data 
in the Reports table. Similarly, when an administrator assigns a task, the system updates the 
Assignments table and links it to the corresponding report and collector. 
The use of structured queries ensures efficient data retrieval and manipulation, allowing the 
system to handle operations such as filtering reports, tracking assignments, and generating 
notifications effectively. 
4.2.3 Authentication and Authorization 
Authentication and authorization are critical components of the system, ensuring that only 
authorized users can access specific functionalities. The system implements user authentication 
through login and registration processes, where user credentials are validated before granting 
access. 
40 
 
Additionally, role-based access control is implemented to differentiate between residents, 
administrators, and waste collectors. Each role has specific permissions, ensuring that users can 
only perform actions relevant to their responsibilities. 
For example, residents can submit reports, administrators can assign tasks, and collectors can 
update task statuses. This structured access control enhances system security and ensures proper 
workflow management. 
4.2.4 Waste Report Submission Process 
The waste report submission process is a core functionality of the system. It allows residents to 
report waste-related issues by providing details such as location, description, and optional 
images. 
When a report is submitted, the system first validates the input data to ensure completeness and 
correctness. It then checks for duplicate reports to prevent redundancy. Once validated, the report 
is stored in the database with a status of ―Pending.‖ 
This process ensures that all submitted reports are properly recorded and made available for 
further processing by administrators. 
4.2.5 Scheduling and Assignment Mechanism 
The scheduling and assignment module is responsible for distributing waste collection tasks to 
collectors. Administrators can view all pending reports and assign them based on priority and 
location. 
When a task is assigned, the system updates the assignment records in the database and changes 
the report status accordingly. This ensures that each report is tracked from submission to 
completion. 
The scheduling mechanism improves efficiency by ensuring that urgent reports are handled 
promptly and that tasks are distributed evenly among collectors.

CHAPTER FIVE 
SUMMARY, CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
5.1 Discussion of Findings 
The findings of this study demonstrate that the Smart Waste Management and Garbage Pickup 
Scheduling System effectively addresses several limitations associated with traditional waste 
management approaches in urban communities such as Buea. 
One of the key findings is that the integration of a web-based reporting platform significantly 
improves communication between residents and waste management authorities. Unlike 
conventional systems that rely on fixed schedules and manual observation, the proposed system 
enables residents to report waste accumulation in real time. This ensures that waste-related issues 
are identified and addressed more promptly. 
Additionally, the implementation of a scheduling mechanism allows waste collection tasks to be 
prioritized based on urgency and location. This represents an improvement over traditional 
collection methods where routes are fixed and do not reflect actual waste conditions. As a result, 
the system reduces inefficiencies such as unnecessary trips to low-priority locations while 
ensuring that critical areas receive timely attention. 
The incorporation of structured task assignment and status tracking further enhances operational 
transparency. Administrators are able to monitor report progress from submission to completion, 
while residents can track the status of their reports. This level of visibility was not present in 
existing systems and contributes to increased accountability. 
Furthermore, the system demonstrates that effective waste management solutions do not 
necessarily require heavy reliance on expensive IoT infrastructure. By leveraging user-generated 
reports and intelligent scheduling, the system provides a practical and cost-effective alternative 
suitable for developing environments. 
Overall, the findings indicate that the system improves efficiency, responsiveness, and 
coordination in waste management processes, thereby contributing to better sanitation outcomes 
and environmental sustainability.

5.2 Conclusion 
This study successfully achieved its primary objective of designing and developing a Smart 
Waste Management and Garbage Pickup Scheduling System. The system provides a practical, 
technology-driven solution to the challenges associated with inefficient waste collection 
processes in urban communities. 
By integrating real-time reporting, intelligent scheduling, and task management functionalities, 
the system enhances communication between residents and waste management authorities while 
improving the overall efficiency of waste collection operations. The implementation results 
demonstrate that the system is capable of addressing key issues such as delayed waste collection, 
inefficient resource utilization, and lack of transparency. 
Unlike many existing solutions that depend heavily on IoT-based infrastructure, the proposed 
system adopts a more accessible and cost-effective approach by utilizing a web-based platform. 
This makes it more suitable for deployment in environments with limited technical 
infrastructure. 
In conclusion, the Smart Waste Management System represents a viable and scalable solution for 
improving urban sanitation and supports the broader goal of sustainable environmental 
management.
